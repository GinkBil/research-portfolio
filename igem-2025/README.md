## Project overview

**LactIQ** was an interdisciplinary synthetic biology project aimed at developing a wearable biosensor for continuous monitoring of lactate in interstitial fluid. Traditional lactate tests require repeated blood samples and provide isolated measurements. The team's proposed approach sought to make lactate information available during exercise, helping athletes and coaches understand training intensity without interrupting a session.

The project combined biological sensing, hardware development, and quantitative approaches to lactate monitoring.

## My contribution: mathematical modeling

I independently developed the mathematical modeling component documented on the team's official iGEM wiki. The work focused on connecting physiological lactate dynamics with practical requirements for a continuous-monitoring sensor.

In particular, the modeling addressed:

- **Lactate dynamics:** predicting how lactate levels fluctuate in the body over time.
- **Sensor requirements:** estimating the minimum detectable lactate concentration required for the proposed monitoring system.
- **Athlete-specific interpretation:** investigating mathematical approaches to refining and personalizing lactate thresholds for training.

The modeling was intended to provide quantitative context for the project's biosensor design and its potential use in endurance training. For the team’s published account of this work, see the [official Model page](https://2025.igem.wiki/uioslo-norway/model).

## Research questions

1. How can changes in lactate concentration over time be represented mathematically?
2. What measurement sensitivity would a continuous sensor need to track physiologically relevant lactate levels?
3. How could model-based interpretation help account for differences in lactate thresholds among athletes?
